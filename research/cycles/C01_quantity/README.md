# Cycle C01 — Quantity as the complete invariant of rewriting

**Branch:** A (objects and transformations without numbers) · **Status:** complete
**Experiments:** EXP-C01-01 … EXP-C01-07 · **Claims:** CLM-C01-01 … CLM-C01-08
**Proofs:** `proofs/lean/Infinity/C01_Quantity.lean`, `proofs/paper/C01.md`

## Step 1 — Hypotheses

Starting point: System A (words over distinguishable symbols, juxtaposition,
rewriting rules; no numbers). Research question: *can quantity be derived
rather than assumed, and what structures does a single transformation rule
create?*

| ID | Hypothesis (as first proposed) | Proposer |
|---|---|---|
| H-C01-1 | Under the swap rules xy ↔ yx the complete invariant is the family of symbol projections s…s (unary numerals). | Inventor |
| H-C01-2 | One relation a^m = a^n on one symbol produces exactly n classes ("counting with a threshold and a cycle"). | Inventor |
| H-C01-3 | Several one-symbol relations: index = min m_i, period = gcd(n_i − m_i). | Explorer |
| H-C01-4 | *(bold)* Any single relation that only rearranges symbols generates full counting equivalence on long words. | Inventor |
| H-C01-5 | *(after a surprise in EXP-C01-04)* If one side u of a length-preserving relation is unbordered, classes ↔ u-avoiding words; generating function 1/(1 − qx + x^k). | Explorer |
| H-C01-7 | *(after exceptions in EXP-C01-05)* In general the class counts depend only on the *common borders* of u and v. | Explorer |
| — | A second operation (multiplication) emerges as composition of substitutions. | Inventor |

## Step 2 — Precise definitions

Words, congruence generated by relations, normal forms, borders and
autocorrelation polynomials are defined in `proofs/paper/C01.md` and
`research/systems/SYSTEMS.md` (System A). "Classes of length n" is exact
because all relations studied preserve length (except the one-symbol ones,
which are handled in N).

## Step 3 — Falsification design

Each hypothesis was paired with an exhaustive finite test that *could* fail:
partitions compared word-by-word (H1), two independent algorithms compared
with the formula on 6159 relation sets (H3), all rearranging relations of
length 3–4 searched for a word whose class is smaller than its counting class
(H4), and exact class counts compared with series coefficients for every
relation over binary k ≤ 6 and ternary k ≤ 4 (H5, H7).

## Step 6 — Analysis

* Counting is *forced*: the only information that survives free rearrangement
  is how often each symbol occurs, and that information is literally a unary
  word. This is the lab's justification for introducing N in System C.
* One-symbol relations produce exactly the "clock with a tail" structures:
  M singleton classes followed by a cycle of length P = gcd of the relation
  gaps. Modular arithmetic is therefore also emergent, not assumed.
* Composition of substitutions *is* multiplication; with two symbols it is
  matrix multiplication and stops being commutative.
* The refuted H-C01-4 led to the most interesting finding of the cycle: the
  number of classes modulo one relation is governed by the *self-overlap
  structure* (borders) of the two sides, exactly as in the theory of
  pattern-avoiding words. The deviating relations are precisely those whose
  sides share a common border; their counts equal avoidance counts of a word
  whose borders are the common borders.

## Step 8 — Comparison with existing mathematics (Historian)

* Theorem 1 is the classical fact that the free commutative monoid is N^Σ
  (Parikh vectors; Parikh 1966); partial commutation leads to Mazurkiewicz
  trace monoids (Cartier–Foata 1969). **REDISCOVERY.**
* Theorems 2–3 are the index/period theory of monogenic semigroups and the
  description of congruences on (N, +) (e.g. Howie, *Fundamentals of Semigroup
  Theory*, 1995). **REDISCOVERY.**
* Theorem 4: End of the free monoid on one generator is (N, ·); standard.
  **REDISCOVERY.**
* Theorem 5 combines Newman's lemma (1942), the critical-pair lemma
  (Knuth–Bendix 1970) and Guibas–Odlyzko (1981); it is the monoid analogue of a
  one-element Gröbner basis (Bergman's diamond lemma, 1978). Very likely
  folklore; novelty **not assessed** pending the Historian audit.
* Conjecture 7: two quick searches found no general formula. Related:
  the survey of one-relation monoids by Nyberg-Brodda
  (arXiv:2105.02853), "Relations on words" (arXiv:1602.03364, which poses
  growth functions of congruences as a natural question), and Kudryavtseva
  (2010; n + 1 classes for a length-2 case). Provisionally **POTENTIALLY
  NOVEL**; flagged for a deeper search (Hilbert series of one-relator algebras:
  Anick, Piontkovski, Iyudu).

## Step 9 — Applications (Application Researcher)

* *Decision procedures from complete invariants.* Equality modulo commutation
  is decided in linear time by counting, versus exploring an exponentially
  large class. Same principle (normal forms) underlies partial-order reduction
  in concurrency verification. No new algorithm is claimed.
* *Information content of an equivalence.* Theorem 5 gives the exact number
  of distinguishable configurations when a single local rewrite is declared
  invisible; e.g. modulo aab ↔ aba binary words carry log₂ φ ≈ 0.694 bits per
  symbol instead of 1. Canonical representatives (u-avoiding words) can be
  enumeratively encoded at that rate. Theoretical only; not benchmarked.
