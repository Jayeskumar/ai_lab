# Cycle C03 — Written proofs: termination of 0^p 1^q → 1^r 0^s

Setting. Words over {0, 1}; the single rule ℓ = 0^p 1^q → ρ = 1^r 0^s with
p, q, r, s ≥ 1; a step is xℓy → xρy. The rule *terminates* if there is no
infinite derivation. φ(w) = complement(reverse(w)) maps derivations of
(p, q, r, s) bijectively to derivations of (q, p, s, r) (since
φ(0^p 1^q) = 0^q 1^p and φ(1^r 0^s) = 1^s 0^r), so termination is invariant
under (p, q, r, s) ↦ (q, p, s, r).

Lean file: `proofs/lean/Infinity/C03_Termination.lean` (standard axioms only).

---

## Theorem 1 (CLM-C03-01) — counting

If s < p or r < q, the rule terminates.

*Proof.* The number of 0s (resp. 1s) drops by p − s (resp. q − r) at every
step. Lean: `terminating_of_count_lt` (any rule decreasing the count of some
letter). ∎

## Theorem 2 (CLM-C03-02) — conservation

If s = p or r = q, the rule terminates.

*Proof.* By the symmetry φ it suffices to treat s = p. Then every step
replaces p consecutive zeros by p consecutive zeros, so the number m of zeros
and their left-to-right order are invariant; number them z_1, …, z_m from the
right. Let N_i be the number of 1s to the right of z_i. In a step whose redex
contains z_j, …, z_{j+p−1}: these zeros lose q ones on their right (N drops by
q), zeros further left see 1^q replaced by 1^r (N changes by r − q), and zeros
further right are unaffected.
Suppose an infinite derivation exists. Claim: every z_i takes part in only
finitely many of its steps. For z_1, N_1 never increases and drops by q each
time z_1 takes part. If z_1, …, z_{i−1} each take part finitely often, only
finitely many steps (K, say) have their redex entirely to the right of z_i; only
those increase N_i, each by at most |r − q|, so N_i ≤ N_i(0) + K|r − q|
forever, while every participation of z_i lowers N_i by q. Hence z_i takes
part finitely often. But every step involves p ≥ 1 of the m zeros, so some
zero takes part infinitely often — contradiction. ∎

*Swap case (Lean).* For (s, r) = (p, q) the number of pairs "0 before 1"
drops by exactly pq per step: `terminating_swap`.

## Theorem 3 (CLM-C03-03) — loops and the doubling region

(a) If u →⁺ x u y for some words x, y, the rule does not terminate (Lean:
`not_terminating_of_loop`, via well-foundedness of the transitive closure and
a minimal element of the set of words containing u).
(b) If r ≥ 2q and s ≥ 2p, the rule does not terminate: with u = 0^p 1^{2q},
u = ℓ1^q → 1^r 0^s 1^q = 1^r 0^{s−p} ℓ → 1^r 0^{s−p} 1^r 0^s
= 1^r 0^{s−2p} · u · 1^{r−2q} 0^s. (Lean: `not_terminating_double`.) ∎

## Theorem 4 (CLM-C03-04) — a loop with r < 2q

0 1 1 → 1 1 1 0 0 (p = 1, q = 2, r = 3, s = 2) does not terminate:
001111 → 01110011 → 0111011100 → 011111100100 → 11100111100100 = 111·001111·00100.
(Lean: `not_terminating_0_11`, every step checked by `decide`.) This refutes the
first guess H-C03-2 ("terminates iff r < 2q or s < 2p"). ∎

## Theorem 5 (CLM-C03-05) — substitution (lifting) lemma

Let σ be a letter-to-word substitution with σ(ℓ) = ℓ′ and σ(ρ) = ρ′. Every step
(and every loop) of ℓ → ρ maps to a step (loop) of ℓ′ → ρ′. (Lean: `step_map`,
`loop_map`.) In particular, with σ(0) = 0^p, σ(1) = 1: a loop of
(1, q, r, k) yields a loop of (p, q, r, kp); with σ(1) = 1^q, σ(0) = 0, a loop
of (p, 1, k, s) yields one of (p, q, kq, s). ∎

## Theorem 6 (CLM-C03-06) — computer-assisted classification (p, q, r, s ≤ 5)

Every rule with p, q, r, s ≤ 5 is classified as stated in
`results/EXP-C03-02.json` and `results/EXP-C03-03.json`, except the rules
listed there as UNKNOWN: count-decreasing and conserving rules by Theorems 1–2
(and, independently, by matrix-interpretation certificates for 218 of the 220
such rules with p, q, r, s ≤ 4 in EXP-C03-01); non-terminating rules by
explicit loop certificates (forward closures, deterministic strategies, lifts
along Theorem 5, mirror images under φ), each re-checked step by step.

*Matrix interpretations (Hofbauer–Waldmann 2006).* Matrices M_0, M_1 over ℕ
with top-left and bottom-right entries ≥ 1 and M_0^p M_1^q ≥ M_1^r M_0^s
entrywise with strict inequality in the top-right entry make the top-right
entry of the product of a word strictly decrease along every derivation. ∎

## Conjecture 7 (CLM-C03-10) — divisibility characterisation

0^p 1^q → 1^r 0^s (p, q, r, s ≥ 1) is non-terminating iff
(a) r ≥ 2q and s ≥ 2p, or
(b) s ≥ 2p, q < r < 2q and p | s, or
(c) r ≥ 2q, p < s < 2p and q | r;
otherwise it terminates.

*Status.* Non-termination in (a) is Theorem 3(b). In (b) it reduces, by
Theorem 5 with σ(0) = 0^p, to the base family (1, q, r, k), k ≥ 2, for which
loops were found for all q ≤ 5, q < r < 2q, k ≤ 6 (EXP-C03-03); (c) is the
mirror image. Termination in the remaining regions is proved where r ≤ q or
s ≤ p (Theorems 1–2); in the region q < r < 2q, p < s < 2p and in the
non-divisible parts of (b)/(c) it is supported only by bounded evidence, apart
from Zantema's problem 0011 → 111000, whose termination is a published theorem
(Geser 1993; Zantema–Geser 1995/2000; RFC match-bounds, Geser–Hofbauer–
Waldmann).

*Relation to the literature.* Zantema and Geser (RTA 1995; AAECC 11, 2000)
give a complete characterization of termination for this family. We could not
access the statement (publisher and repository pages were not retrievable), so
we cannot yet say whether Conjecture 7 coincides with it; this must be checked
before any claim beyond "rediscovery" is made.
