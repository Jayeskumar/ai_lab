# Cycle C05 — Written proofs and proof notes

Conventions. A magma (M, ∗) of finite order n; L_a(x) = a∗x, R_a(x) = x∗a.
An identity t = x (t a term in x, y) is *nontrivial* if x occurs in t but is
neither the leftmost nor the rightmost leaf of t. (If x is absent, the
identity forces |M| = 1; if x is the leftmost (rightmost) leaf, the left
(right) projection a∗b = a (= b) is a model of every order.) The *spectrum*
of an identity is the set of orders of its finite models. ETP numbers refer
to the Equational Theories Project list (teorth.github.io/equational_theories).

---

## Lemma 0 — the SAT encoding is exact (systems/magma.py)

Variables v[a,b,c] (a∗b = c) with exactly-one per cell; for each compound
subterm s and each assignment σ of its variables, variables w[s,σ,c] with
clauses w[s₁,σ,a] ∧ w[s₂,σ,b] ∧ v[a,b,c] → w[s,σ,c] (leaves replaced by their
assigned values) and at-most-one over c; finally both sides of each identity
are forced to take equal values.

*Claim.* The satisfying assignments, projected to v, are exactly the Cayley
tables satisfying the identities. *Proof.* Given a table satisfying the
identities, set every w to the true value of its subterm: all clauses hold.
Conversely, in a satisfying assignment, by induction on subterms the
linking clauses force w[s,σ,·] to contain the true value of s under σ, and
at-most-one makes it the only true w; the final equality clauses then state
the identities. ∎ (Cross-checked against brute force on all magmas of
orders 2 and 3 for four identities: identical model counts.)

## Lemma 1 (CLM-C05-05) — translations along the path to a unique occurrence of x

Let t = x hold in a finite magma, where x occurs exactly once in t. Along the
path from the root of t to that leaf, each node contributes a translation
L_u (if the path goes right and the left sibling evaluates to u) or R_u (if
it goes left). The siblings do not contain x, so for fixed values of the
other variables the composition T₁∘T₂∘…∘T_k of these translations is the
identity map. Hence T_k is injective and T₁ surjective, and inductively every
T_i is a bijection (a map between finite sets of equal size that is injective
or surjective is bijective). In particular, for x = y∗s(x, y) every left
translation L_y is bijective (Lean: `Infinity.C05.left_mul_bijective_of_law`,
which needs only surjectivity of L_y and finiteness, so it applies even when
x occurs several times in s). ∎

This is the sound implied constraint ("every row is a permutation") added to
the SAT instances of EXP-C05-03 for laws of the form x = y∗(…).

## Theorem 2 (CLM-C05-04) — law 167, x = (y∗x)∗(x∗y): orders are ≡ 0, 1 (mod 4)

*Proof.* Machine-checked: `Infinity.C05.law167_card_mod_four`. Let
F({a, b}) = {a∗b, b∗a} on unordered pairs. The law gives
(a∗b)∗(b∗a) = b and (b∗a)∗(a∗b) = a, so F∘F = id. If a ≠ b then a∗b ≠ b∗a
(otherwise F(F{a,b}) = {c∗c} would be a singleton), so F preserves the set
of the C(n,2) pairs of distinct elements; and F has no fixed point there
(both cases {a∗b, b∗a} = {a, b} contradict the law). A fixed-point-free
involution forces C(n,2) to be even, i.e. 4 | n(n − 1). ∎

*Sufficiency* (n ≡ 0, 1 mod 4 all occur) is due to the ETP write-up
"Spectra of order-3 equations" (B. Gunby-Mann), Proposition 3; we only add
machine-certified models of orders 4 and 5 (`T4_law167`, `T5_law167`).

## Refutations

* **H-C05-1** ("every identity t = x has spectrum {1} or all n"): law 167 has
  models of orders 4 and 5 (certified tables) but none of orders 2, 3, 6, 7
  (Theorem 2). Refuted.
* **H-C05-Q** ("every nontrivial identity t = x forces its finite models to be
  quasigroups"): the 4-element model T4 of law 167 has a repeated entry in
  row 1 (`T4_not_latin`). Refuted.

## Proposition 3 — Mendelsohn family (rediscovery)

ETP law 66, x = y∗(x∗(y∗y)), has spectrum {n ≡ 0, 1 mod 3} \ {6}
(ETP write-up "Spectra of order-3 equations", Proposition 2; models
correspond to Mendelsohn triple systems; Mendelsohn 1971). Our census
reproduces this pattern on n ≤ 10 for law 66 and for every identity found
to have the same small models (EXP-C05-01/02); `no_law66_order2` certifies
the absence of order-2 models in Lean.

## Lemma 4 — soundness of the extra SAT constraints (EXP-C05-03, -04, -06)

(a) *Rows are permutations* for laws x = y∗(…): Lemma 1 / Lean
`left_mul_bijective_of_law`. (b) *Symmetry break 0∗0 ∈ {0, 1}*: if a model
on {0, …, n−1} has 0∗0 = a ∉ {0, 1}, relabel by the transposition π = (1 a);
π fixes 0, the relabelled table is an isomorphic model (identities and the
row-permutation property are invariant under isomorphism), and its value at
(0, 0) is π(a) = 1. Hence adding (a) and (b) neither creates nor destroys
the existence of a model of order n. ∎

**How memberships and gaps are certified.** A membership n ∈ spec(law) is
certified by an explicit Cayley table checked by Lean's kernel (`decide`),
independently of the SAT solver (EXP-C05-04). A gap n ∉ spec(law) is
certified by a DRUP refutation of the CNF of Lemma 0 + Lemma 4, produced by
Glucose 4 and checked by drat-trim (EXP-C05-06); the trust base is then
drat-trim, the CNF generator (Lemma 0, cross-checked against brute force)
and Lemma 4.
