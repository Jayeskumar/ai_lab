# Cycle C05 — Written proofs and proof notes

Conventions. A magma (M, ∗) of finite order n; L_a(x) = a∗x, R_a(x) = x∗a.
An identity t = x (t a term in x, y) is *nontrivial* if x occurs in t but is
neither the leftmost nor the rightmost leaf of t. (If x is absent, the
identity forces |M| = 1; if x is the leftmost (rightmost) leaf, the left
(right) projection a∗b = a (= b) is a model of every order.) The *spectrum*
of an identity is the set of orders of its finite models. ETP numbers refer
to the Equational Theories Project list (teorth.github.io/equational_theories).

---

## Lemma 0 — the SAT encoding is exact (systems/magma.py)

Variables v[a,b,c] (a∗b = c) with exactly-one per cell; for each compound
subterm s and each assignment σ of its variables, variables w[s,σ,c] with
clauses w[s₁,σ,a] ∧ w[s₂,σ,b] ∧ v[a,b,c] → w[s,σ,c] (leaves replaced by their
assigned values) and at-most-one over c; finally both sides of each identity
are forced to take equal values.

*Claim.* The satisfying assignments, projected to v, are exactly the Cayley
tables satisfying the identities. *Proof.* Given a table satisfying the
identities, set every w to the true value of its subterm: all clauses hold.
Conversely, in a satisfying assignment, by induction on subterms the
linking clauses force w[s,σ,·] to contain the true value of s under σ, and
at-most-one makes it the only true w; the final equality clauses then state
the identities. ∎ (Cross-checked against brute force on all magmas of
orders 2 and 3 for four identities: identical model counts.)

## Lemma 1 (CLM-C05-05) — translations along the path to a unique occurrence of x

Let t = x hold in a finite magma, where x occurs exactly once in t. Along the
path from the root of t to that leaf, each node contributes a translation
L_u (if the path goes right and the left sibling evaluates to u) or R_u (if
it goes left). The siblings do not contain x, so for fixed values of the
other variables the composition T₁∘T₂∘…∘T_k of these translations is the
identity map. Hence T_k is injective and T₁ surjective, and inductively every
T_i is a bijection (a map between finite sets of equal size that is injective
or surjective is bijective). In particular, for x = y∗s(x, y) every left
translation L_y is bijective (Lean: `Infinity.C05.left_mul_bijective_of_law`,
which needs only surjectivity of L_y and finiteness, so it applies even when
x occurs several times in s). ∎

This is the sound implied constraint ("every row is a permutation") added to
the SAT instances of EXP-C05-03 for laws of the form x = y∗(…).

## Theorem 2 (CLM-C05-04) — law 167, x = (y∗x)∗(x∗y): orders are ≡ 0, 1 (mod 4)

*Proof.* Machine-checked: `Infinity.C05.law167_card_mod_four`. Let
F({a, b}) = {a∗b, b∗a} on unordered pairs. The law gives
(a∗b)∗(b∗a) = b and (b∗a)∗(a∗b) = a, so F∘F = id. If a ≠ b then a∗b ≠ b∗a
(otherwise F(F{a,b}) = {c∗c} would be a singleton), so F preserves the set
of the C(n,2) pairs of distinct elements; and F has no fixed point there
(both cases {a∗b, b∗a} = {a, b} contradict the law). A fixed-point-free
involution forces C(n,2) to be even, i.e. 4 | n(n − 1). ∎

*Sufficiency* (n ≡ 0, 1 mod 4 all occur) is due to the ETP write-up
"Spectra of order-3 equations" (B. Gunby-Mann), Proposition 3; we only add
machine-certified models of orders 4 and 5 (`T4_law167`, `T5_law167`).

## Proposition 3 — Mendelsohn family (rediscovery)

ETP law 66, x = y∗(x∗(y∗y)), has spectrum {n ≡ 0, 1 mod 3} \ {6}
(ETP write-up "Spectra of order-3 equations", Proposition 2; models
correspond to Mendelsohn triple systems; Mendelsohn 1971). Our census
reproduces this pattern (orders 1, 3, 4, 7 present; 2, 5, 6, 8 absent) on
n ≤ 8 for law 66 and the three other identities with the same pattern
(EXP-C05-01/02; non-memberships solver-trusted there); `no_law66_order2`
certifies the absence of order-2 models in Lean. The two order-4 identities
sharing the pattern on n ≤ 6 (ETP 1083 and its mirror) do *not* hold in
Mendelsohn quasigroups and do have models of order 8.

## Lemma 4 — soundness of the extra SAT constraints (EXP-C05-03, -04, -06)

(a) *Rows are permutations* for laws x = y∗(…): Lemma 1 / Lean
`left_mul_bijective_of_law`. (b) *Symmetry break 0∗0 ∈ {0, 1}*: if a model
on {0, …, n−1} has 0∗0 = a ∉ {0, 1}, relabel by the transposition π = (1 a);
π fixes 0, the relabelled table is an isomorphic model (identities and the
row-permutation property are invariant under isomorphism), and its value at
(0, 0) is π(a) = 1. Hence adding (a) and (b) neither creates nor destroys
the existence of a model of order n. ∎

**How memberships and gaps are certified.** A membership n ∈ spec(law) is
certified by an explicit Cayley table checked by Lean's kernel (`decide`),
independently of the SAT solver (EXP-C05-04). A gap n ∉ spec(law) is
certified by a DRUP refutation of the CNF of Lemma 0 + Lemma 4, produced by
Glucose 4 and checked by drat-trim (EXP-C05-06); the trust base is then
drat-trim, the CNF generator (Lemma 0, cross-checked against brute force)
and Lemma 4.

## Remark 5 — the order-9 model of law 467 is covered by the notes

Le Floch's notes state that law 467, x = y∗(x∗(x∗(y∗y))), holds on
ℤ[i]/(k + il) with x∗y = −((k² + l² + 1)/2)(1 + i)x + iy whenever k² + l² is
odd and k, l have different parities. The pair (k, l) = (3, 0) is allowed and
gives ℤ[i]/(3) ≅ 𝔽₉ with x∗y = (1 + i)x + iy (as −5 ≡ 1 mod 3). Check, mod 3:
y∗y = (1 + 2i)y; x∗(y∗y) = (1 + i)x + (i − 2)y = (1 + i)(x + y);
x∗(x∗(y∗y)) = (1 + i)x + (i − 1)(x + y) = 2i·x + (i − 1)y;
y∗(x∗(x∗(y∗y))) = (1 + i)y − 2x − (1 + i)y = −2x = x. ∎
So the Lean-certified table `E467_order9_law` (EXP-C05-04) confirms a size
already implied by the notes; it is not counted as new in CLM-C05-07.

## Theorem 6 (CLM-C05-10) — linear models over finite fields (computer-assisted)

*(Added after the Skeptic's review, which refuted CLM-C05-09 with models of
order 16.)* Let q be a prime power and x∗y = ax + by with a, b ∈ GF(q). By
induction on terms, every term t(x, y) evaluates to α_t x + β_t y with
α_x = 1, β_x = 0, α_y = 0, β_y = 1 and
α_(s∗u) = aα_s + bα_u, β_(s∗u) = aβ_s + bβ_u. Hence the law x = t holds in
(GF(q), ∗) iff α_t(a, b) = 1 and β_t(a, b) = 0 — two polynomial equations in
(a, b). EXP-C05-07 evaluates them for all q² pairs and every prime power
q ≤ 128 (GF(p^k) built as GF(p)[t]/(f) with f the first monic irreducible of
degree k; since GF(q) is unique up to isomorphism the answer does not depend on
f). One model per law and q is re-checked on its full q × q table (corrected
after the Skeptic's review of batch 1.1, which re-checked every solution
independently, reviews/skeptic_batch1.1.md §3.1), and the tables
E677_order16, E1313_order16 and E670_order9 are checked by Lean's kernel
(`decide`). A model of order q is a spectrum member; the absence of a linear
model says nothing about other models. ∎

*Consequence.* Laws 677 and 1313 have models of order 16, so CLM-C05-09
(spectrum {1} ∪ {odd n ≥ 5}) is false; the Skeptic's examples are
x∗y = (1 + t³)x + t³y (677) and x∗y = t³x + (1 + t³)y (1313) over
GF(2)[t]/(t⁴ + t + 1).

## Lemma 7 — finite models of law 1313 are quasigroups (Lean)

*(Batch 1.1, for EXP-C05-08.)* In a finite magma satisfying law 1313,
x = y∗(((y∗x)∗x)∗y), every left translation L_y is a bijection (it is onto,
by the law, and the magma is finite), and so is every right translation
R_y: z ↦ z∗y. Indeed, given w put x = y∗w; the law gives
y∗(((y∗x)∗x)∗y) = x = y∗w, so ((y∗x)∗x)∗y = w by injectivity of L_y, and R_y
is onto. Lean: `law1313_left_bijective`, `law1313_right_bijective`
(proofs/lean/Infinity/C05_Law1313.lean). For law 677 only the left
translations are known to be bijective (`law677_left_bijective`); finite models
of 677 that are not right-cancellative exist (ETP blueprint, chapter on
equation 677, section 13.1), so no column constraint is used for 677. Neither
law holds in the right projection x∗y = y on two or more elements
(`law1313_not_right_projection`, `law677_not_right_projection`). ∎

## Lemma 8 — a complete case split by the row of one element

Let M be a magma of order n whose left translations are all bijections. For a
partition λ of n and a part ℓ of λ, let π(λ, ℓ) be the permutation of
{0, …, n−1} whose cycles, written on consecutive labels, have the lengths of λ,
the first of them of length ℓ and containing 0. Write fix(a) for the number of
fixed points of L_a.

1. If M is idempotent (a∗a = a for all a), choose a with fix(a) minimal.
2. Otherwise choose a with a∗a ≠ a and fix(a) minimal among such elements.

Let λ be the cycle type of L_a and ℓ the length of its cycle through a; ℓ = 1
in case 1 (a∗a = a) and ℓ ≥ 2 in case 2. Two permutations with the same cycle
type are conjugate by any bijection that maps cycles onto cycles of the same
length and respects the cyclic order, and such a bijection can be chosen to map
a to 0 (send the cycle of a onto the first cycle of π(λ, ℓ), starting at 0).
Relabel M by it: the relabelled magma M′ has L′_0 = π(λ, ℓ). Relabelling
preserves idempotency and the numbers fix(·), so M′ satisfies:

* case 1: every element is idempotent and every row has at least
  λ.count(1) = fix(0) fixed points;
* case 2: every non-idempotent element's row has at least λ.count(1) fixed
  points.

Hence a law whose models have bijective left translations has a model of
order n iff one of the CNFs (idempotent, λ, 1) with 1 ∈ λ, or
(non-idempotent, λ, ℓ) with ℓ ≥ 2 a part of λ, is satisfiable; each CNF
contains the law, "rows are permutations", row 0 = π(λ, ℓ) and the
constraints above. For n = 8 there are 15 + 30 = 45 CNFs, for n = 10
30 + 67 = 97. The encoding "a∗a = a or at least k of the entries a∗b = b"
adds the literal a∗a = a to every clause of a cardinality encoding of the
second disjunct. ∎

## Lemma 9 — implied division clauses

Write the law as x = y∗T. Since L_y is injective, y∗a = x forces T = a. For
677, T = x∗S with S = (y∗x)∗y, and L_x is injective, so y∗b = x and x∗a = b
force S = a. For 1313, T = S∗y with S = (y∗x)∗x, and R_y is injective
(Lemma 7), so y∗b = x and a∗y = b force S = a. These clauses are implied by
the law and the bijectivity of the translations, so adding them changes no
answer; they only shorten the search. ∎

## Lemma 9′ — the division clauses in general (batch 1.2)

`systems/magma_split.py` generalises Lemma 9 to every law x = y∗T. For each
assignment (x, y) and each node N = A∗B of T on the path from the root, with
N(x, y) = t:

* if A is a variable (value a), then a∗b = t forces B(x, y) = b, because L_a is
  injective (rows are permutations, Theorem 6.3 of the textbook);
* if B is a variable (value b) and the columns are bijective (Lemma 7′, 7″, or
  the quasigroup part of a split case), then a∗b = t forces A(x, y) = a, because
  R_b is injective.

The descent starts at the root with y∗a = x ⇒ T(x, y) = a and continues into the
factor so determined while one of the two rules applies. Every clause is a
consequence of the law and of the injectivity of the translations used, so
adding them changes no answer. ∎

## Theorem 10 (CLM-C05-11, -12, -13) — laws 677 and 1313 at orders 8 and 10 (computer-assisted)

No magma of order 8 or 10 satisfies law 677, x = y∗(x∗((y∗x)∗y)), or law
1313, x = y∗(((y∗x)∗x)∗y). Together with the sizes already decided (EXP-C05-03,
-06, the ETP notes), the orders n ≤ 10 of the models of each law are exactly
1, 5, 7 and 9.

model of order n exists iff one of the CNFs (idempotent, λ, 1) or

refutes all 284 of them with CaDiCaL 1.5.3; each refutation is a DRAT proof

## Theorem 14 (CLM-C05-22) — the spectrum of law 501 is exactly the orders n ≡ 0, 1 (mod 4)

Law 501, x = y∗(y∗(x∗(x∗y))), has a model of order n ≥ 1 if and only if
n ≡ 0 or 1 (mod 4).

*Necessity.* Let M be a finite model, n = |M|. The law makes each left
translation L_y onto, hence a permutation. Put x□y := x∗(x∗y). The left
translation of □ at x is L_x², an even permutation, and the law reads
x = y□(x□y): (M, □) satisfies ETP law 14 (it is *semi-symmetric*).

1. In (M, □) the right translation R□_y : x ↦ x□y is injective (x = y□(x□y)
   recovers x from x□y), hence a permutation, and L□_y ∘ R□_y = id. So
   R□_y = (L□_y)⁻¹ is even as well.
2. With z = x□y, law 14 at (y, z) gives y = z□(y□z) = z□x. Hence the
   permutation Φ(x, y) = (y, x□y) of M × M satisfies Φ²(x, y) = (x□y, x) and
   Φ³(x, y) = (x, (x□y)□x) = (x, y): every cycle of Φ has length 1 or 3, so
   sgn Φ = +1.
3. Φ = τ ∘ P with P(x, y) = (x□y, y) and τ(x, y) = (y, x). P acts on each set
   M × {y} as R□_y, so sgn P = ∏_y sgn R□_y = +1 by step 1, and therefore
   sgn τ = +1. But τ fixes the n pairs (x, x) and exchanges the others in
   n(n − 1)/2 transpositions, so sgn τ = (−1)^{n(n−1)/2}.

Hence n(n − 1)/2 is even, that is, n ≡ 0 or 1 (mod 4). (Steps 1–3 prove more
generally that ∏_y sgn L□_y = (−1)^{n(n−1)/2} in every finite semi-symmetric
quasigroup.) Lean: `law501_card_mod_four`, with `sign_prodComm` for the sign of
the swap (proofs/lean/Infinity/C05_Law501.lean, standard axioms only). The identity
is a corollary of the classical parity relation π_r + π_c + π_s ≡ C(n, 2) (mod 2)
for Latin squares (Janssen 1995; Zappa 1996; see Francetić, Herke and Wanless,
JCTA 155 (2018), arXiv:1703.04764): in a semi-symmetric quasigroup the row,
column and symbol permutations have the same parity. The proof above is a direct
one for this case.

*Sufficiency.* Let A be an abelian group of order n and x□y = −x − y. Then
y□(x□y) = −y + x + y = x, so (A, □) satisfies law 14, and each translation
L□_x : y ↦ −x − y is an involution. An involution is the square of a
permutation iff it has an even number of 2-cycles: (a c b d)² = (a b)(c d), and
conversely the 2-cycles of a square come in pairs from the 4-cycles of its root.
The fixed points of L□_x are the solutions of 2y = −x, which form either the
empty set or a coset of A[2] = {a : 2a = 0}.

* n odd, A = Z_n: |A[2]| = 1, so L□_x has one fixed point and (n − 1)/2
  2-cycles, an even number when n ≡ 1 (mod 4).
* n ≡ 0 (mod 4), A = Z_2 × Z_2 × Z_{n/4}: L□_x has either no fixed point and
  n/2 2-cycles, or f = |A[2]| fixed points, with f = 4 if n/4 is odd and f = 8
  if n/4 is even, and (n − f)/2 2-cycles; in each case the number is even
  (n − f ≡ 0 mod 8).

Choose for each x a square root σ_x of L□_x and put x∗y := σ_x(y). Then
x∗(x∗y) = x□y, and y∗(y∗(x∗(x∗y))) = y□(x□y) = x. ∎

*Computations (EXP-C05-15).* The construction is carried out for every
n ≡ 0, 1 (mod 4) up to 100 and the law is checked on each of the 50 tables; the
sign identity of the remark holds on all 39 semi-symmetric quasigroups of orders
3–9 sampled by a SAT search; the case split also finds a model of order 12
(idempotent, not a quasigroup). The certified gaps of 501 at orders 2, 3, 6, 7
(EXP-C05-06, -12) and 10 (EXP-C05-09) are instances of the theorem, and so is the
order 11 that the case-split probes could not settle.

*Literature (reviews/historian_501.md).* No source read states the mod-4
restriction, the existence for all n ≡ 0, 1 (mod 4), or a model of order 12; the
ETP commentary on 501 (September 2026) still calls its finite spectrum unknown.

## Theorem 15 (CLM-C05-24) — law 677 has no quasigroup model of order 12 (computer-assisted)

No quasigroup of order 12 satisfies law 677, x = y∗(x∗((y∗x)∗y)). Hence every
model of order 12, if one exists, is not right-cancellative; it lies in one of
51 explicit CNFs, and it is either idempotent with a row having exactly one fixed
point, or has a non-idempotent element a with the fewest fixed points whose row
has at most one fixed point and moves a in a cycle of length at least 4.

*Proof.* Every finite magma with Latin rows either has Latin columns as well or
has a value missing from some column; the two parts of a case CNF
(`systems.magma_split.build`, part = "quasigroup" / "non-quasigroup") add the
corresponding constraints, and in the quasigroup part also the column division
clauses of Lemma 9′. By Proposition 13 it suffices to treat the 57 cases left by
EXP-C05-11 (their CNFs are rebuilt and their SHA-256 hashes checked). EXP-C05-13
refutes all 57 quasigroup parts (at most 276,023 conflicts each; 13.2 million
proof lines) and 6 of the 57 non-quasigroup parts — the six hard non-idempotent
cases whose row 0 has exactly two fixed points (5.4 million proof lines in the
non-quasigroup runs) — each by a DRAT proof that drat-trim verified. The other 51
non-quasigroup parts (14 idempotent, 37 non-idempotent) were undecided after
1,000,000 conflicts each. ∎

*Literature (reviews/historian_677_nonqg.md).* No complete or quasigroup-only
search of 677 at order 12 is reported; searches for counterexamples to 255 at
sizes 12–16 never terminated (J. Hyun, ETP Zulip, October 2025). The smallest
known model of 677 that is not right-cancellative has order 25 (M. Reinke,
November 26, 2025), so a model of order 12 would be a new smallest one.

*Further probes (not recorded).* For the hardest non-quasigroup part (idempotent,
row 0 of cycle type (11, 1)), neither a split by the missing pair (column,
value) up to the symmetries that fix row 0, nor the implied clauses
"a∗x = b∗x = u ⇒ a∗u ≠ b∗u" (for a ≠ b; from (y∗x)∗y = x\(y\x)) decided it within
1,000,000 conflicts.
