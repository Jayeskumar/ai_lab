# Quantity from transformation {#ch-quantity}

*Volume 1 — Foundations and alternative mathematical objects. Research cycle C01.*

## Motivation

Can numbers be *derived* rather than assumed? We start with nothing but
distinguishable symbols, the empty word, juxtaposition, and rules that declare
two words interchangeable. If quantity is real, it should appear as whatever
survives the transformations — an *invariant* — and the invariant should be
forced, not chosen.

## Definitions

Let $\Sigma$ be a finite alphabet of $q$ symbols. A *relation* $u \leftrightarrow v$
allows replacing a factor $u$ by $v$ (or back) anywhere in a word. The
*congruence* generated by a set of relations identifies two words when one
can be transformed into the other by finitely many such replacements. An
*invariant* is a map that is constant on congruence classes; it is *complete*
if it separates different classes.

A *border* of a word $w$ is a nonempty proper prefix of $w$ that is also a
suffix; $w$ is *unbordered* if it has none. The *autocorrelation polynomial*
of $w$ ($|w| = k$) is $c_w(x) = 1 + \sum_{b} x^{k-|b|}$, the sum over the
borders $b$ of $w$.

## Counting emerges

**Theorem 2.1** (counting is the complete invariant of commutation)
*[PROVEN — Lean: `commEq_iff_count`; REDISCOVERY]*. Modulo the relations
$xy \leftrightarrow yx$ ($x, y \in \Sigma$), two words are congruent if and
only if every symbol occurs equally often in both. The projection of a word
onto a symbol $s$ — delete all other symbols — is the unary word $s\cdots s$.

So the only information that survives free rearrangement is a family of
unary words, one per symbol. This is the laboratory's justification for
introducing the natural numbers: they are the classes of the one-symbol free
monoid, and they *are* the invariant.

**Theorem 2.2** (one symbol: a clock with a tail) *[PROVEN — Lean:
`gen_iff`, `exactly_m_add_p_classes`; REDISCOVERY]*. On words over one symbol
$a$, the relation $a^m = a^{m+p}$ ($p \ge 1$) produces exactly $m + p$
classes: $a^x \sim a^y$ iff $x = y$, or $x, y \ge m$ and $x \equiv y \pmod p$.

**Theorem 2.3** *[PROVEN — written; REDISCOVERY]*. Several relations
$a^{m_i} = a^{n_i}$ generate index $M = \min m_i$ and period
$P = \gcd(n_i - m_i)$. (Two independent algorithms agree with this on 6159
relation sets, EXP-C01-02.)

Modular arithmetic is therefore also emergent: it is what one relation does to
counting.

**Theorem 2.4** (multiplication is composition) *[PROVEN — written;
REDISCOVERY]*. The substitutions $a \mapsto a^k$ of the one-symbol free monoid,
with pointwise juxtaposition as addition and composition as multiplication,
form a commutative semiring isomorphic to $(\mathbb{N}, +, \times)$. With two
symbols modulo commutation, composition is matrix multiplication and is no
longer commutative.

## A bold conjecture fails

*Conjecture (H-C01-4) [REFUTED].* Any single relation that only rearranges
symbols — like $aab \leftrightarrow aba$ — eventually identifies all words with
the same symbol counts.

*Counterexample.* Under $aab \leftrightarrow aba$, the word $baa$ contains
neither side, so its class is $\{baa\}$, while $aab, aba, baa$ have the same
counts. All 33 rearranging relations of length 3 and 4 fail in the same way
(EXP-C01-04).

## An unexpected Fibonacci pattern

The failed experiment produced something better than a confirmation. For each
relation, count the classes of words of length $n$ over $\{a, b\}$:

| relation | $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ |
|---|---|
| all six length-3 relations | 2, 4, 7, 12, 20, 33, 54, 88, 143, 232, 376 |
| 25 of 27 length-4 relations | 2, 4, 8, 15, 28, 52, 96, 177, 326, 600, 1104 |
| $aaba \leftrightarrow abaa$ (and complement) | 2, 4, 8, 15, 28, 52, **97**, 181, 338, 631, 1178 |

The first row is $F_{n+3} - 1$ (partial sums of Fibonacci numbers), the second
the partial sums of tribonacci numbers. The explanation is the self-overlap
structure of the words involved.

**Theorem 2.5** (border containment) *[PROVEN — written, Skeptic-reviewed;
the list identities of the critical-pair join are checked in Lean
(`common_border_joinable`); REDISCOVERY: Kurth 1987]*. Let $u \neq v$ have length $k$ and suppose
every border of $u$ is a border of $v$. Then the rewriting system
$\{u \to v\}$ terminates and is confluent; the classes of length-$n$ words
correspond to the words avoiding $u$; and
$$\sum_{n \ge 0} \#\{\text{classes of length } n\}\, x^n
  = \frac{c_u(x)}{x^k + (1 - q x)\, c_u(x)} .$$
For unbordered $u$ this is $1/(1 - qx + x^k)$; for $q = 2$ the coefficients
are partial sums of $(k-1)$-bonacci numbers.

*Proof sketch.* Every step changes the word in the same lexicographic
direction inside a finite set, so derivations terminate. Two overlapping
occurrences of $u$ overlap along a border $b$ of $u$; since $b$ is also a
border of $v$, writing $u = pb = bs$ and $v = p'b = bs'$ one checks
$vs = p'u \to p'v$ and $pv = us' \to vs' = p'v$, so the critical pair joins in
one step on each side. Newman's lemma gives confluence, and the count of
$u$-avoiding words is the Guibas–Odlyzko formula. ∎

The exceptional relations are those whose two sides *share* a border. Testing
that observation led to a precise conjecture.

**Conjecture 2.6** (common borders) *[COMPUTATIONALLY SUPPORTED; partly
settled by the literature; POTENTIALLY NOVEL only in the case "common border
without containment"]*. For all $u \neq v$ of length $k$, the class-count series is
$c(x)/(x^k + (1-qx)c(x))$ with
$c(x) = 1 + \sum_{b} x^{k - |b|}$, the sum over the words $b$ that are borders
of *both* $u$ and $v$.

*Evidence.* No counterexample among all 2159 relations with binary $k \le 6$
and ternary $k \le 4$ (up to $n = 12$, resp. $7$), including 1326 relations
not covered by Theorem 2.5 (EXP-C01-06).

**Theorem 2.7** *[PROVEN — computer-assisted; REDISCOVERY for 1620 of the
1632 relations]*. Conjecture 2.6 holds for all
$n$ for 1632 of these relations. *Method:* Knuth–Bendix completion finds a
finite convergent system (independently re-verified: rules decrease, all
critical pairs join, $u$ and $v$ have equal normal forms); its irreducible
words are counted by an automaton with $s$ states, so the count sequence obeys
a recurrence of order $\le s$; the conjectured series obeys one of order $k$;
equality of the first $s + k + 5$ terms therefore implies equality forever
(EXP-C01-07). Every certificate is stored with the experiment, every completed
rule is re-derived from $u = v$ by search, and the Skeptic re-verified all 1632
certificates with independently written code. The remaining 527 relations have infinite completions, e.g.
$aba \leftrightarrow bab$ completes to $bab \to aba$ together with
$b a^j b a \to a b a a b^{j-1}$ for all $j \ge 2$.

### What the literature already knew

Our Historian's independent audit found that much of this territory had been
mapped by a different route — homological algebra of one-relator monoids:

* Theorem 2.5 is the length-preserving case of Kurth's (1987) one-rule
  confluence criterion, and Kurth's criterion is an *iff*: $u \to v$ is
  confluent exactly when every border of $u$ is a border of $v$ (we confirmed
  this on 28,850 pairs, EXP-C01-08).
* When $u$ and $v$ share **no** border, Conjecture 2.6 follows from a 2026
  theorem of Gray and Steinberg together with Anick's theory of strongly free
  relations:

  **Theorem 2.8** *[PROVEN — corollary of published theorems, every step
  checked by the Skeptic; REDISCOVERY]*. If $u \ne v$ of length $k$ have no
  common border, the class-count series is $1/(1 - qx + x^k)$.

  *Proof idea.* No common border means the monoid is "incompressible", so it
  has Hochschild cohomological dimension at most 2 (Gray–Steinberg 2026,
  Thm 8.1); the trivial module of $K\langle\Sigma\rangle/(u - v)$ then has
  the resolution $0 \to A(-k) \to A(-1)^q \to A \to K \to 0$, whose Euler
  characteristic is the formula. ∎ Every one of the 527 relations that our
  completion method could not settle lies in this case, so all 2159 relations
  of EXP-C01-06 are now proved.
* With exactly **one** common border, growth identities read off from
  Gray–Steinberg (2022) imply the conjectured formula *provided* a certain
  multiplication map on a left ideal is injective. The Skeptic found that
  this lemma was asserted but not proved; it holds in all 2591 cases tested,
  so the case is "proved modulo one lemma".

What remains open is the case of two or more common borders without
containment. The formula holds in every case tested: the Skeptic's search
covered 295,358 relation orbits and 1,491 targeted relations with one to four
common borders, up to $n = 27$, without a single failure. The episode is recorded as a lesson:
two quick web searches were not enough to establish novelty.

## Comparison with existing mathematics

Theorems 2.1–2.4 are classical: Parikh vectors (1966) and trace monoids
(Cartier–Foata 1969), monogenic semigroups, the endomorphism semiring of
$\mathbb{N}$. Theorem 2.5 is Kurth (1987) with Guibas–Odlyzko (1981). For
Conjecture 2.6 see the previous section: the no-common-border case is a
corollary of Gray–Steinberg (2026) and Anick (1982), and the homological
counterpart of the whole common-border dichotomy is Gray–Steinberg (2022,
Theorem B). The explicit growth formula with common borders was not found.

## Applications and limitations

Complete invariants turn equivalence testing from a search into a computation
(counting decides commutation-equivalence in linear time). Theorem 2.5 also
measures how much information a single "invisible" local rewrite destroys:
modulo $aab = aba$, binary words carry $\log_2 \varphi \approx 0.694$ bits per
symbol instead of 1. These are observations, not benchmarked algorithms.
